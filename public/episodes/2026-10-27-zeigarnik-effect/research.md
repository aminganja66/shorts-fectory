# Research: Zeigarnik effect (2026-10-27-zeigarnik-effect)

## Verified facts
1. Zeigarnik published the study in 1927 (Psychologische Forschung). Main experiment: 32 adults, 22 tasks (thread winding, multiplication, drawing...), about half interrupted. Interrupted tasks were recalled roughly 2x as often as completed ones (her own conclusion, "approximately twice as well"). The exact 1.9 ratio comes only from a secondary essay, NOT verified in the original tables, so say "लगभग दोगुना".
   - https://www.simplypsychology.org/zeigarnik-effect.html
   - https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2019/07/23/why-cant-we-forget-what-weve-left-undone/ (quotes her conclusion; page returned 403 to me, quote via search snippet)
   - Original English translation: https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/willpower/1927-zeigarnik.pdf
2. Replication problem (NOTE: the key meta-analysis is Ghibellini and Meier 2025, not 2016; I found no 2016 paper). "Interruption, recall and resumption: a meta-analysis of the Zeigarnik and Ovsiankina effects", Humanities and Social Sciences Communications 12, 2025. Finding: NO memory advantage for unfinished tasks overall, but a general tendency to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina effect). Abstract: "The memory advantage of unfinished tasks, however, has proven particularly difficult to replicate."
   - https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w
   - https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html
   - Popular blogs cite d = 0.15, 59 studies, ratio 0.99: UNVERIFIED, do not use.
3. Older mixed replications: Hovland (1951) wrote "few investigators could unequivocally reproduce Zeigarnik's findings"; Butterfield (1964), Van Bergen (1968) found it inconsistent; Seifert and Patalano (1991) "Memory for incomplete tasks: A re-examination of the Zeigarnik effect", Cognitive Science Society proceedings pp. 114-119, argued cognitive conditions (type of interruption, set size, processing time) matter. Safe statement: "बाद की कई studies में नतीजे मिले-जुले रहे".
   - https://www.simplypsychology.org/zeigarnik-effect.html
   - https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2qb9x9wd
4. Masicampo and Baumeister (2011), "Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals", JPSP 101(4), 667-683. Unfinished goals caused intrusive thoughts during an unrelated reading task, higher accessibility of goal words and worse anagram performance; letting people make a specific plan eliminated these effects (for the goal-related thoughts, not by completing the goal). Caveat: same authors later found rigid plans can reduce openness to alternatives (Social Cognition 2012).
   - https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024192
   - https://roybaumeister.com/2012/06/16/committed-but-closed-minded-when-making-a-specific-plan-for-a-goal-hinders-success/
5. Origin anecdote: Kurt Lewin (Zeigarnik's supervisor) reportedly noticed a waiter remembering unpaid orders and forgetting them after payment; usually set in a Berlin café (some retellings say Vienna). This is an ANECDOTE, possibly apocryphal; details (jacket, exact orders vs running total) vary. Say "कहा जाता है / कहानी के मुताबिक", attribute the observation to Lewin, not Zeigarnik.
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeigarnik_effect
   - https://www.simplypsychology.org/zeigarnik-effect.html

## Everyday examples (illustrative, not claims of study results)
Series cliffhanger that makes you want the next episode; pending email/assignment that pops up at bedtime. Frame as "अक्सर ऐसा लगता है", not as proven by the 1927 memory study (meta-analysis does not support the memory part; the pull to resume is the better-supported part).

## Practical tip (verified)
Write a specific plan: when/where you will do the unfinished task. Masicampo and Baumeister 2011 found this reduced the intrusive thoughts and interference in their experiments. Not medical advice; not a cure for anxiety.

## Do NOT claim
- "Unfinished tasks are remembered 90% better / 1.9x" as settled fact today, or "scientifically proven".
- That the Zeigarnik memory effect is robust/replicated. Say original 1927 result, later replications mixed, 2025 meta-analysis found no memory advantage.
- Any numbers from blogs (d = 0.15, 59 studies, 0.99).
- Waiter story as fact, "Berlin café" as certain, or that Zeigarnik herself saw the waiter.
- That plan-making means the brain "finishes" the task, any ADHD/anxiety/depression/insomnia diagnosis or treatment claims.
- "5 minutes of starting" trick as research-backed (the example storyboard has it; I verified no source for it. Use the plan tip instead).
- Ovsiankina effect is a separate (Maria Ovsiankina, 1928) tendency to resume; fine to mention as "resume करने की खिंचाव" per the 2025 meta-analysis.
