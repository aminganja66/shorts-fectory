## Facts & sources
- In December 1975 Kodak engineer Steven Sasson built a self-contained digital camera prototype: about 8 lb (3.6 kg), toaster-sized, black and white, 100x100 pixels (0.01 megapixel), saved to a cassette tape. — https://spectrum.ieee.org/first-digital-camera-history, https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9261340
- Recording one photo to tape took 23 seconds (about as long again to play it back on a TV). Sasson got a US patent in 1978; the camera was never put into production. — https://spectrum.ieee.org/first-digital-camera-history, https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9261340
- Reportedly, Kodak executives saw no market for it after several demos, and Sasson was not allowed to show it outside Kodak. Sasson later said demos drew curiosity and some annoyance. No verified direct quote from a named manager; do not quote 'cute, but don't tell anyone'. — https://spectrum.ieee.org/first-digital-camera-history, https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna9261340
- Kodak's global workforce peaked at about 145,300 in 1988. By 1996 it had 94,800 employees and about $16.0 billion in sales ($15,968M). — https://rbj.net/2012/01/19/kodaks-growth-and-decline-a-timeline/, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000031235/000003123597000001/0000031235-97-000001.txt
- Kodak did go digital: pocket-sized consumer digital cameras in 1996 and the 'You've Got Pictures' service with AOL in 1998. It still filed for Chapter 11 on January 19, 2012, with about $5.1 billion in assets and about $6.75 billion in liabilities (Bloomberg rounds to $6.8B). — https://rbj.net/2012/01/19/kodaks-growth-and-decline-a-timeline/, https://www.bloomberg.com/news/2012-01-19/kodak-photography-pioneer-files-for-bankruptcy-protection-1-.html
- Facebook agreed to buy Instagram for $1 billion, announced April 9, 2012, about three months after Kodak's filing. Instagram was reported to have about a dozen employees (13 in some outlets). — https://money.cnn.com/2012/04/09/technology/facebook_acquires_instagram/index.htm, https://www.npr.org/transcripts/150335237
