# Research: Kodak / Sasson digital camera (brand, EN)

## Facts (see storyboard.json facts[])
1. Dec 1975 prototype: ~8 lb / 3.6 kg, toaster-size, B&W, 100x100 px = 0.01 MP, cassette tape. (IEEE Spectrum, NBC, Wikipedia)
2. 23 s to record (about 23 s to play back). US patent 1978, never produced. (IEEE Spectrum, NBC)
3. Reportedly execs saw no market after several demos; Sasson barred from showing outside Kodak. Sasson later said: curiosity and some annoyance. Famous "cute, but don't tell anyone" line: only secondary sources, wording varies, NYT original not retrievable - do NOT quote.
4. Kodak global employment peak 145,300 in 1988 (Rochester Business Journal timeline). 1996: 94,800 employees, sales $15,968M (SEC 10-K FY1996).
5. Digital efforts: 1996 pocket digital cameras, 1998 AOL "You've Got Pictures". Chapter 11 on Jan 19, 2012; ~$5.1B assets, ~$6.75B liabilities (Bloomberg rounds to $6.8B).
6. Instagram: $1B by Facebook, April 9, 2012; ~a dozen employees (13 in some reports); ~30M users.

## Do NOT claim
- That Kodak "never entered digital" or "destroyed" the camera. It patented it, kept it internal, later sold digital cameras. IEEE/Wikipedia: Sasson himself disagreed that Kodak was just slow.
- Any direct quote from a named Kodak executive. Not verified.
- "145,000 employees" in the same breath as 1996 revenue; the peak was 1988. Say "at its 1988 peak, about 145,000 people".
- "Kodak had X% market share" in 1975/76: not verified (only 1996 US share 80.1% to 74.7% over 1996-97 seen in one secondary source; skip).
- Instagram exact headcount as 13 without "reported". Safer: "about a dozen".
- Instagram sold *because* of Kodak; just a contrast, 3 months apart.
- Sasson "invented the first digital camera" is fine as "built the first self-contained digital camera prototype" (digitalkameramuseum notes nuances).
